Why Play review says the item you were attempting to purchase could not be found

AppContinuity, solo developer · Published 22 November 2026

The sandbox and Play internal testing article is about proving a purchase with license testers, accelerated renewals, and View Sandbox Data turned on. That test is necessary. It is not the test Google’s reviewer runs. A Capacitor paywall can unlock on your device, then fail Play review with a Broken Functionality rejection and a screenshot of Google’s purchase sheet: “The item that you were attempting to purchase could not be found.”

This piece is about that gap on this stack — React + Capacitor, Supabase Auth, Stripe on the web, RevenueCat on iPhone and Android — and the checks that catch it before you submit again. Identity, Stripe attach, and webhook handling are covered in the earlier articles. This one is the Play catalog and the purchase the reviewer actually attempts.

The failure mode

The pattern is consistent:

  • Yearly and weekly (or monthly) subscriptions are Active in Play Console. Purchases work for you, and for license testers on an internal or closed track.
  • You submit a production release. The reviewer often still sees prices on the paywall.
  • They tap Subscribe. Play’s own sheet opens, then says the item could not be found. The rejection may also call the button unresponsive, or say it leads to an error page.
  • An appeal with a screen recording of your own purchase can still be denied. The reviewer was never on the license-tester path you recorded.

That is usually not a Capacitor click handler. It is a product or offer that Play will not sell to that account in that country, even though the same paywall works for testers in yours.

What the error actually is

The message appears on Google Play’s purchase sheet, not in a RevenueCat dialog. You cannot catch it and replace it with your own copy; the sheet has already appeared. Google’s Play Billing docs describe the matching condition as ITEM_UNAVAILABLE: the product is not available for purchase for this user. They tell you to refresh product details, only sell products that queryProductDetailsAsync returned, and check eligibility and regional configuration. To be available, a product needs to be active, its app needs to be published, and the app needs to be available in the user’s country. They also note a propagation delay, especially during testing: the configuration can be correct and the error can still appear until Play’s servers catch up.

RevenueCat’s Capacitor SDK sits on top of that same Play Billing call. When you purchasePackage, Play is the one that accepts or refuses the product or offer.

Every base plan and offer has its own country list

It is not enough that “the yearly plan is available everywhere.” Google documents that users can only purchase subscriptions in the regions where that base plan is available, and that availability is decided by the user’s Google Play country. Each base plan has its own region list and its own prices. Offers inherit the base plan’s regions by default, and you can restrict an offer to a subset of those regions.

In Play Console, under Monetize with Play, open each subscription, then each base plan and each offer. Confirm it is Active, available in every country you distribute the app, and has a price in each of those countries. If you added a weekly plan later, or a free trial only in some regions, that is the plan the reviewer may be buying. If you did not opt into “New countries/regions,” a country Play added later will not have the plan.

The same rule applies to the app itself. If the production track is not available in the reviewer’s country, Google’s ITEM_UNAVAILABLE guidance still applies: the app has to be available there too.

Offer eligibility, and what not to hardcode

Google has three eligibility criteria for offers: new customer acquisition, upgrade, and developer determined. For the first two, Play decides who qualifies. RevenueCat documents that ineligible offers do not appear in a StoreProduct’s subscriptionOptions. Developer-determined offers always appear, and you are responsible for when to apply them.

If you pass a package to the purchase call, the RevenueCat SDK automatically applies an available free trial or introductory offer: the longest eligible free trial, else the cheapest eligible intro, else the base plan. Offers tagged rc-ignore-offer are skipped. Developer-determined offers are included in that automatic pick unless you tag them. That default is usually what you want on a normal paywall. It becomes a problem when a custom paywall hardcodes an offer ID or an offer token that Play did not return for this account — for example an offer that is not available in the reviewer’s country, or a cached “new customer acquisition” offer from a different session. Purchase that, and Play’s sheet can fail even though the base plan would have sold.

Only sell what the store returned

Build the paywall from Purchases.getOfferings() and render only packages on offerings.current.availablePackages — the list RevenueCat already filtered to products the store returned on this device. If current is null or the list is empty, do not show a Subscribe button that will fail. Show a short “Subscriptions aren’t available right now” message and a retry. Put a timeout on the loading spinner. A spinner that never ends, or a button that only opens an error sheet, is what reviewers describe as unresponsive.

RevenueCat’s displaying-products guide is the same: check that current is not null and availablePackages is not empty before you display packages, and do not hardcode product IDs or free-trial text. In Capacitor that looks like this:

import { Purchases } from '@revenuecat/purchases-capacitor';

const offerings = await Purchases.getOfferings();
const packages = offerings.current?.availablePackages ?? [];
if (packages.length === 0) {
  // Show a retry. Do not call purchasePackage.
  return;
}

// Render only these packages, then:
const purchaseResult = await Purchases.purchasePackage({
  aPackage: selectedPackage,
});

If the paywall is behind Supabase Auth, give the reviewer a test account in Play Console’s App access instructions so they can reach it. A sign-in wall with no credentials can look like a broken button before Play’s sheet ever opens.

Reproduce it as a reviewer would

License testers are the happy path. They get test payment methods, they can sideload, and your publishing account is already one of them. A Play reviewer is not on that list.

Google’s own billing test docs say you should also test with non-license-tester accounts, and that users incur actual charges for test-track purchases unless they are license testers. That is the reproduction:

  1. Use a Google account that is not on your license-tester list.
  2. Add it to a testing track and open the opt-in URL on the device.
  3. Install from the Play Store link, not a sideload.
  4. Sign in to the Capacitor app, call Purchases.logIn with the Supabase user.id, and try to buy each package the paywall shows.
  5. If the buy succeeds, refund the order in Play Console under Manage account > Play apps order management. Use refund and revoke if you need the entitlement cleared.

If the sheet fails for this account and works for a license tester, the catalog — regions, offers, eligibility — is the problem, not your click handler. This is a real charge, so do it once, on a cheaper plan if you have one, and refund it. The account’s Play country is what Play uses to decide which base plans are for sale; making every distributed country complete is more reliable than trying to guess the reviewer’s locale.

Make Play and RevenueCat agree

Customers never purchase a Google Play subscription product directly. They purchase a base plan, or an offer on that plan. RevenueCat products map to those base plans. For products set up after February 2023, the identifier format is subscriptionId:basePlanId. If you renamed, deactivated, or recreated a base plan, an offering can still point at the old one.

Confirm the identifiers in the RevenueCat product catalog match what is Active in Play Console. The SDK also logs a configuration warning when none of the dashboard products could be fetched from Play. If you recently published a new product or changed regional availability, wait for propagation before you treat a failure as a code bug. RevenueCat documents that newly released products may take up to 24 hours; Google documents the same class of delay for ITEM_UNAVAILABLE.

After a rejection

Fix the catalog and the paywall first. Then submit again, with a short note for the reviewer: what changed, how to sign in, and how to reach the paywall. A recording of a license-tester purchase can help explain the setup, but it does not prove a regular Play account in another country can buy. Repeating an appeal with the same catalog and the same paywall usually leaves the reviewer on the same failing purchase.

Short checklist

  • Confirm each base plan and each offer is Active, with a price, in every country the app is distributed — including “New countries/regions” if you want new Play countries covered.
  • Confirm the production track is available in those same countries.
  • Do not hardcode offer IDs or tokens. Purchase a package from getOfferings(), or a subscriptionOption the store returned for this user.
  • If you use developer-determined offers and automatic offer selection, tag offers you do not want auto-applied with rc-ignore-offer.
  • If offerings.current is null or availablePackages is empty, show an error and a retry; do not show a dead Subscribe button. Time out the spinner.
  • If the paywall is behind Auth, put a test account in App access.
  • Reproduce with a non-license-tester account on a test track, install from the opt-in link, and refund the real charge if it succeeds.
  • Match RevenueCat product IDs to Play’s subscriptionId:basePlanId form for each active base plan.
  • After catalog changes, allow time for Play to propagate before you resubmit.
  • A license-tester success is not a reviewer success. The tester path is in testing subscriptions in App Store sandbox and Play internal testing.
